Katarina Andrade

🔊Hear my name

Olá! I’m a Brazilian performer based in Chicago, bringing curiosity and humor to stage and screen.

Wait a minute… who are you?

Katarina Andrade (using the quick Latino tongue tap R) is a Theatre and Physics & Astronomy senior at Northwestern University, a blend of art and science. She hosts and narrates the night sky for visitors at Dearborn Observatory, where storytelling meets stargazing. She has played leading roles both on screen and on stage, starring as no one but herself in "Autobiografia," an autobiographical short film exploring her own life, and as the revered Virgin Mary in O Auto da Compadecida (A Dog's Will), the classic Brazilian play by playwright Ariano Suassuna.

Beyond acting, Katarina brings that same attention to detail to set design, including Northwestern's 93rd Waa Mu Show, "Taken Away: A Musical Trilogy," at Cahn Auditorium. True to her reputation, nothing on her set actually got taken away, and it stayed rock solid, anchoring three one-act musicals connected by themes of loss and grief.

She is also a proud mezzo-soprano, with Northwestern's all-female Significant Others a cappella group, fulfilling her lifelong dream of being a real-life Barden Bella from Pitch Perfect (beatboxing skills still a work in progress). She also dances Brazilian funk and choreographs for Dale Duro Latin Dance Company because, in her words, "nothing builds friendship quite like shaking ass together."

The other half of her brain is dedicated to astrophysics, with research on galaxies and black holes at Northwestern's Center for Interdisciplinary Exploration and Research in Astrophysics (CIERA). Her stargazing has taken her as far as the top of the world, Hawaii's Mauna Kea, the tallest mountain on Earth (sorry, Everest), where the constellations look nothing like the ones back home in Brazil.
